# Design Rationale

## Why the web

Mira's personal website is both the place the player visits and the object the player repairs. Its address bar, broken links, old image formats, and surviving page fragments make the browser a believable archive instead of a neutral container around a story.

## Why failure is a puzzle mechanic

Broken images and missing routes create small invitations to investigate. Repairing the photograph asks the player to notice a person; revealing a message changes the interpretation of the missing data; comparing versions makes provenance visible. Glitches mark transitions and uncertainty without turning into constant spectacle.

## Why the archive records the player

The visitor record mirrors the archive's own behavior: restoration attempts and opened files become a second record assembled through play. It is explicitly fictional, counts only local game state, and never collects identity or uploads activity. The twist asks the player to notice the difference between observing a trace and claiming ownership of it.

## Why three endings

Restore Everything produces a convincing whole that includes unsupported inference. Preserve Verified Fragments keeps dated evidence while labeling unknowns. Respect the Deletion stops recovery of material Mira asked to remove. Each is a different archival policy with a cost; none is presented as the single correct answer.

## Memory, truth, and autonomy through interaction

The player first experiences recovery as satisfying work. Later, the source comparison interrupts the assumption that a coherent reconstruction is necessarily true, and the signed request reveals that absence can be intentional. The final write is an action rather than a quiz answer: the player decides what this archive will do with uncertainty and consent.
